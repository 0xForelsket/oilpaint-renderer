# L3 report: the Rust planner (cutover)

Milestone L3 of `docs/plans/LIBRARY_PLAN.md` (v4), 2026-09-30.

**Status: acceptance measured; waiting for Sean's side-by-side sign-off.** After the sign-off, one dedicated commit
retires v1's C kernel, its Python kernel wrapper and its Python planner. That commit also re-points the eval
harness at the `oil` CLI. Both are the last step of L3 and are not done yet.

Machine: Windows 11 laptop, Intel Core Ultra 7 258V, rustc 1.91.1 MSVC, Node 24.11.1. Everything below is
*measured* unless marked *projected*. Timings are single-thread, pinned to the four performance cores, and
interleaved across hosts.

## Summary

- **The planner is in Rust** (`crates/oil-plan`). It is a cutover from v1's Python planner, as Sean decided: the
  same ideas, restructured, with no parity gate. It adds:
  - flows evaluated at stroke points;
  - one random stream per (layer, region);
  - neutral defaults with style presets;
  - stroke variety;
  - photos as targets;
  - pick-up per distance.
- **Replay is exact.** A StrokeList painted at its plan width reproduces the planner's proxy canvas bit for bit in
  every plane (test).
- **Determinism holds on 7 hosts.** Storm Light's and the still life's StrokeLists are byte-identical on native
  Windows, Node, Chromium, Firefox, WebKit, Chrome and Edge. `golden/2.0.0-dev.3.json` (31 cases) is the first
  golden of this engine version.
- **Planning is 3x faster than v1.** Storm Light at 600 px takes 7.6 s native and 15.2–15.6 s in WASM (Chromium,
  Node); v1's Python planner takes 22–25 s on the same kernel.
- **The acceptance scenes pass every sanity gate.** Both scenes are planned end to end from TypeScript, natively and
  in WASM, with identical bytes.
- **Against v1 (information, not a gate):** stroke counts per layer are within a few percent, and the look is the
  same scene with more varied strokes.

**The planner's design** (details in `spec/SCENEPLAN_V1.md`, "Planning"):
- **Sites:** error cells (0.6 x mean + 0.4 x max Lab difference, with a re-check), density grids and curves
  (boundary tracing or polylines), each restricted to the region mask's box. Then painterly sweep order.
- **Paths** follow the region's flow at points: angle jitter, curvature, wobble, a flick, and stopping where the
  soft mask fades.
- **Colour** is a reference sample or a palette colour, then palette snapping through the mixer, Lab jitter and
  floor, flecks and warmth.
- **Proxy canvas.** It is painted exactly as `oil_paint` replays. A Lab cache with dirty tiles serves the error
  planes (the incremental error planes of plan section 3).

## Against v1 (information)

Both plans are painted by the same `oil paint` (Ochrell, 600 px, seed 1907), so only the planner differs.
`tools/compare_plans.py` writes the full tables.

- **Strokes per layer:** within a few percent (13,486 v1, 13,439 engine). The largest differences are "Sea
  underpainting" (1,361 against 1,668) and "Tower and lamp" (3,526 against 3,336).
- **Widths** are about the same. **Lengths** are shorter (lengthSkew, dabs): the sky's median length is 0.206 cw
  in v1 and 0.182 cw in the engine.
- **Image metrics per region:** similar.
  - Edges are softer: `edge_step_p99` is lower in 6 of 7 regions (sky 4.3 → 2.9, glow 3.8 → 2.3).
  - The near sea carries more relief (`h_p95` 32 → 44), from the preset's thick-lights rule.
  - Hairlines and chroma are about equal.
- **Two-point stubs:** paths stopped after one step are 4.8% of strokes, against 4.9% in v1. This is inherited, and
  a candidate for L9.

## Decisions I took (reversible, for the record)

1. **One random stream per (seed, layer, seed offset, region).** Editing a region leaves every other region's
   strokes alone; v1 shared one stream per layer.
2. **Palette snapping uses the effective colours,** including a layer's override. v1 snapped to the region
   style's palette even when the layer overrode `colors`.
3. **The scumble height blur runs once per layer,** as the painter does. v1 recomputed it for each region, so its
   proxy drifted from the replay.
4. **Error cells cover the whole canvas,** including partial cells at the right and bottom edges; v1 skipped them.
5. **Contour flows use a smooth minimum of the edge distances** (0.01 cw) instead of a blurred distance raster.
   `waves` measures depth to the exact canvas bottom.
6. **Neutral defaults:** `lFloor` 0 (the impressionist preset keeps 20) and `splay` 0.35 (v1's 1.0 read as pencil
   lines).
7. **Stroke-variety values in the impressionist preset** are first guesses, not tuned: `sizeByDetail` 0.4,
   `lengthSkew` 0.6, `dabShare` 0.15, `endVariation` 0.3, `flick` 0.15, `reliefByValue` 0.3.

## What failed or is open

**Found and fixed:**
- **My first spacing test for pick-up passed on the old kernel too,** because it measured where pick-up had settled.
  The final test measures the smear after a wet patch and fails on the old kernel.
- **The CLI usage text** had silently missed its L2 update (a replace without an assert); fixed.
- **The first width check measured stroke points,** not drawn widths.

**Concurrent work.** Another session edited `oil-mix` and added `oil-palette` in the middle of L3, and its
half-finished crate broke the workspace build. At Sean's choice I waited until it was committed (`8a31338`).

**Open:**
- **The retirement commit, after sign-off.** It removes:
  - `oilpaint/csrc` (the C kernel);
  - `oilpaint/_build.py` and `oilpaint/canvas.py` (the kernel wrapper);
  - `oilpaint/planner.py`, `render.py` and the Python-only tools and tests that need them.

  It also re-points the eval harness (sheet and scene runs) at `oil plan` and `oil paint --npy`, which already
  exist, together with the new StrokeList reader. That is about 20 files.
- **Toolbox exports** (plan section 3). The Rust stages are public (`Planner::plan_layer`, the site proposers,
  `Palette`, `Rng`), but only the composed `plan` is exported through WASM and TS. The typed-array toolbox handles
  belong with the WASM host (L4).
- **Stroke variety is untuned.** The keys work (tested through the replay test), and their preset values need a
  visual pass, best done with L7's lighting.
- **The still life shows the realism roadmap's gaps:** pill-shaped wall strokes and flat modelling (L7, L9).
